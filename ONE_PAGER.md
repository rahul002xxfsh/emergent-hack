## Idea Breakdown

### Product
**What are we building (one sentence):** A voice-first web app where you pick a parody celebrity therapist (Donald Trump, Snoop Dogg, Amitabh Bachchan or Narendra Modi), vent about everyday stress out loud, hear funny in-character advice spoken back, and get a "Session Prescription" card to download and share.

### Problem
**The problem (2–4 sentences: what hurts today, how often, what it costs):** Students and young professionals deal with small, daily stress (deadlines, exams, dating, procrastination) that is too minor for real therapy but too heavy to keep bottled up. Venting to friends wears thin, and generic AI chatbots answer in the same flat, polite voice, so venting feels like filling in a form. The result is unprocessed stress and no fun way to let it out.
**How often it happens:** Daily to weekly: every deadline, exam week, bad date or lost afternoon.
**What it costs them today:** Time lost to procrastination and stress spirals, and the social cost of offloading the same complaints on the same friends.
**Existing alternatives and the gap:** Friends (tired of hearing it), journaling (no reply), generic chatbots (helpful but bland, no personality, nothing worth sharing), real therapy (expensive and overkill for everyday stress). None of them make venting fun or give you something to show friends.
**Evidence:** _ (to collect: quick poll of 20+ classmates on how often they vent and to whom; add 1–2 quotes)

### User
**Who is affected (target user / persona):** College students and young professionals (18–30) who are online, meme-literate, and stressed by exams, work and deadlines. Example: an MBA student at 11 PM, three assignments due, who just wants to rant and laugh.
**Who benefits and how much:** The user gets a 2-minute laugh plus 3 small, concrete steps to act on; their friends get a funny card to share, which brings new users in.

### Your Solution
**What it is (plain language):** You choose a celebrity parody therapist, talk (or type) about what's stressing you, and they answer in their voice and style: Trump calls your to-do list a bad deal to renegotiate, Snoop tells you to ride the deadline like a beat. When you end the session you get a Session Prescription card with a diagnosis, a 3-step prescription and a signoff.
**How it solves the problem (each pain point → the feature that fixes it):**
- Pain: venting to bots feels flat → Feature: 6 distinct personas with their own voice settings, catchphrases and comedic style
- Pain: typing out feelings is effort → Feature: speak your rant; hear the reply read aloud
- Pain: venting leads nowhere → Feature: Session Prescription card with 3 concrete, funny steps
- Pain: nothing worth sharing → Feature: one-tap download / share of the prescription card
**Why this approach over the obvious alternative:** The obvious alternative is a "nice" AI wellness chatbot; those already exist and nobody talks about them. Comedy plus a shareable card turns venting into something users want to repeat and show friends, which makes it both useful and viral.
**Why it's different (key insight):** People don't need a lecture for small stress, they need to laugh at it. A familiar character roasting your problem makes it feel smaller, and the card gives the moment a shareable ending.
**Business / sustainability model:** Free with 3 personas; ₹49–99/month for all personas, unlimited sessions and new seasonal personas. Branded personas for colleges and companies later.

### User Flow
(how will it work for a user)

1. User does: opens the app and picks a persona from the persona grid.
2. System does: speaks the persona's greeting from the "therapy couch".
3. User does: taps the mic (or types) and vents.
4. System does: replies in character, as text plus voice.
5. User does: continues for 2–4 turns, then taps "End Session".
6. System does: generates the Session Prescription card (diagnosis, 3-step Rx, signoff).
7. User gets: a funny, shareable card; can download or share it, or try another persona.

**Demo script (3 minutes):**
1. Show the problem (~20s): "It's 11 PM, three deadlines, and your friends have muted you." Ask the room who has procrastinated today.
2. Walk the core flow, start to finish (~90s): pick Snoop Dogg → greeting plays → vent out loud about procrastination → in-character voice reply → one follow-up → End Session.
3. Show the "wow" moment (~30s): the Session Prescription card appears; download it; quickly switch to Amitabh Bachchan with the same rant to show the persona difference.
4. Impact and what's next (~20s): shareable card = free growth; next: more personas and challenge-a-friend links.

(eg payments, complex stuff)

## Engineering

**Will your product have login? (Yes/No):** No (an optional login is a good-to-have if time permits)
**Will your product involve AI Models?** Yes: one LLM (via Emergent's built-in LLM integration) for in-character replies and for writing the Session Prescription.
**Will your product involve external services?** Only the browser's built-in Web Speech API (speech-to-text and text-to-speech). No maps, email or payments.

### What is your tech stack?
Pls go through the deck + resources and give a simplistic answer to this - avoid GPT/Claude fluff

- **Frontend:** React web app (built by Emergent) — why: mobile-friendly UI, quick to build
- **Backend:** Emergent-generated backend API — why: keeps the LLM key off the browser and holds the persona prompts
- **Database:** Emergent's default database — why: store sessions and prescriptions for the share link; small and simple
- **AI / ML:** One LLM with a persona system prompt — why: one model, 4 personalities, defined in `personas.json`
- **Hosting / deployment:** Emergent deploy — why: one click, no setup
- **APIs / services:** Web Speech API (SpeechRecognition + speechSynthesis) — why: free, runs in the browser, no extra keys

**Architecture:**
```
[User] → [React frontend + Web Speech mic/voice] → [Backend API] → [Database]
                                                       ↓
                                              [LLM + persona prompt]
```
- **Frontend component:** responsibility, inputs, outputs: shows persona grid and chat; turns voice into text and reply text into speech; sends messages to the backend and renders the card.
- **Backend component:** responsibility, inputs, outputs: takes persona id + chat history, adds the persona system prompt, calls the LLM, returns the reply; on End Session asks the LLM for the prescription as JSON.
- **Key design decisions and trade-offs:** browser voices instead of voice cloning (free, legal, fast); one LLM with 6 prompts instead of 6 models; no login to keep the demo path short; built-in crisis rule that drops the comedy and shows a helpline (Tele-MANAS 14416).

**Data the app stores (data model):**
- Persona entity: key fields id, name, title, style tag, greeting, system_prompt, catchphrases, voice_config ; notes: static, from `personas.json`
- Session entity: key fields id, persona_id, messages, created_at ; notes: no user identity stored
- Prescription entity: key fields session_id, diagnosis, three_step_rx, signoff ; notes: shown on the card

**API endpoints (if any):**
- `GET /api/personas` — list all personas (auth: none)
- `POST /api/chat` — persona_id + messages in, in-character reply out (auth: none)
- `POST /api/prescription` — session messages in, prescription JSON out (auth: none)

**Project structure:**
```
couch-parody/
├── frontend/      # React app: persona grid, chat, prescription card
├── backend/       # API: chat + prescription endpoints, personas.json
└── docs/          # diagrams, deck, screenshots
```

**Running it:**
- Prerequisites: built and run inside Emergent; API keys needed: Emergent's LLM key (managed by Emergent)
- Environment variables: `LLM_API_KEY` — LLM access ; `DATABASE_URL` — sessions store
- Run command: `run / preview in Emergent` (runs at the Emergent preview URL)

**Testing:** how to run: click through the full demo flow on the deployed app on the presentation phone/laptop ; what is tested: all 4 personas reply in character, mic works, card generates every time ; manual test cases (scenario → expected result): rant about exams with Trump → "rigged game you'll win bigly" reply + card ; type a self-harm message → comedy stops and helpline shows ; deny mic permission → typing still works.

**Deployment:** where it is hosted: Emergent ; how to redeploy: Emergent deploy button after a tested checkpoint.

**Scalability:** what breaks at 10× load and how we'd fix it: LLM cost and rate limits break first → cache greetings, cap turns per session, use a smaller model for chat.
**Ethics, privacy and accessibility:** Clear "parody, entertainment only, not real therapy" label; persona prompts ban politics, real controversies and offensive content; crisis messages trigger a helpline instead of jokes; no login and no personal data stored; typing works as an alternative to voice; captions shown for every spoken reply.

**Challenges and how we solved them:**
- Challenge: personas drifting into generic AI tone → Solution: strict system prompts with cadence, vocabulary and "never break character" rules
- Challenge: parodying real living people safely → Solution: topic limits in prompts (no politics or real controversies) plus a parody label
